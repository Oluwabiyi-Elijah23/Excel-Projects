# 🍕 Pizza Sales Analysis Dashboard

![Pizza Sales Dashboard](../Excel%20Projects%20Images/Pizza%20Sales%20Project%20Images/Home_Dashboard.png)

## Project Overview

This project focuses on analyzing pizza sales data using Microsoft Excel, Power Pivot, DAX, Pivot Tables, and Interactive Dashboards.

The objective of the project was to transform raw transactional data into meaningful business insights by answering key business questions such as:

- What is the total revenue generated by the business?
- What is the average amount spent per order?
- What are the daily, monthly, and hourly trends of customer orders?
- What pizza categories and pizza sizes contribute the most to revenue?
- Which pizza products perform best and worst based on revenue, quantity sold, and total orders?

The project was divided into four major phases:

1. Data Cleaning & Transformation
2. Data Modeling & KPI Development
3. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
4. Dashboard Development

# Phase 1: Data Cleaning & Transformation

The first step in the project was to create a duplicate copy of the original dataset. This ensured that the original dataset remained untouched while data cleaning and transformation activities were performed on the copied version.

## Data Type Standardization

The data types of the various columns were reviewed and adjusted appropriately:

- Numeric fields were converted from General to Number
- Text fields were converted from General to Text
- The Order Date column was converted to Date
- The Order Time column was converted to Time

This ensured consistency and improved the reliability of subsequent calculations and analyses.

## Pizza Size Standardization

The pizza size column originally contained abbreviated values.

Using Excel's Find and Replace functionality, the abbreviated values were converted to more user-friendly names:

| Original Value | Updated Value |
|----------|----------|
| L | Large |
| M | Medium |
| S | Regular |
| XL | X-Large |
| XXL | XX-Large |

This transformation improved readability and made the dashboard easier for end users to interpret.

## Table Creation

The cleaned dataset was converted into an Excel Table using:

`CTRL + T`

Converting the dataset into a table improved data management, filtering, and integration with Power Pivot.

## KPI Results

### Total Revenue

The total revenue generated by the business was:

**$817,860.05**

This metric represents the total amount spent by customers across all pizza orders.

It is important to note that revenue does not represent profit, since production costs and operating expenses have not been deducted.

---

### Average Order Value

The average amount spent per order was:

**$38.31**

This metric was calculated by dividing the total revenue by the total number of unique orders.

```text
Average Order Value = Total Revenue ÷ Total Orders
```

This indicates that customers spent approximately $38 on each order on average.

---

### Total Pizzas Sold

The total number of pizzas sold during the period under review was:

**49,574 Pizzas**

This metric was calculated by summing the Quantity field across all transactions.

---

### Total Orders

The total number of orders placed was:

**21,350 Orders**

This metric was calculated using a Distinct Count of the Order ID field.

A distinct count was necessary because a single order could contain multiple pizza items.

---

### Average Pizzas Per Order

The average number of pizzas purchased per order was:

**2.32 Pizzas**

This metric was calculated using:

```text
Average Pizzas Per Order = Total Pizzas Sold ÷ Total Orders
```

This indicates that customers typically purchased between two and three pizzas per transaction.

---

## KPI Insights

Several business insights can be drawn from the KPI analysis:

- The business generated over **$817,000** in revenue during the period under review.

- Customers spent an average of **$38.31** per transaction.

- More than **49,000 pizzas** were sold across **21,350 orders**.

- Customers purchased approximately **2.32 pizzas per order**, suggesting that many customers ordered multiple pizzas in a single transaction.

- The combination of a relatively high average order value and multiple pizzas per order may indicate group purchases, family orders, or shared meals.

# Phase 3: Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

After developing the KPI measures, the next step was to perform Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) to identify trends and patterns within the pizza sales data.

The chart requirements section focused on understanding customer purchasing behavior across different days, months, and hours, as well as identifying the contribution of different pizza categories and sizes to overall sales performance.

---

## Daily Trend of Total Orders

The first question in this section was to determine the daily trend of total orders.

To answer this question, a DAX calculated column was created using the **FORMAT** function to extract the day names from the Order Date field. A Pivot Table was then inserted from the Data Model and a Distinct Count of Order ID was performed for each day of the week.

### Daily Trend of Total Orders

### Results

| Friday | 3,538 |
| Thursday | 3,239 |
| Saturday | 3,158 |
| Wednesday | 3,024 |
| Tuesday | 2,973 |
| Monday | 2,794 |
| Sunday | 2,624 |

### Key Insights

- Friday recorded the highest number of orders with 3,538 orders.
- Thursday and Saturday also recorded strong performance, indicating increased customer demand towards the end of the week.
- Order activity was generally higher between Wednesday and Saturday compared to Sunday through Tuesday.
- The results suggest that customer demand tends to increase as the weekend approaches.
- Businesses can use this information to better plan staffing levels, inventory purchases, and promotional campaigns during peak periods.

## Percentage of Sales by Pizza Category

The next analysis focused on determining the percentage contribution of each pizza category to total revenue.

|---------------|-------------------:|
| Classic | 26.91% |
| Supreme | 25.46% |
| Chicken | 23.96% |
| Veggie | 23.68% |

### Key Insights

- The Classic category contributed the highest percentage of total revenue at 26.91%.
- The Supreme category closely followed with 25.46%.
- Revenue contribution was relatively balanced across all pizza categories.
- The difference between the highest and lowest performing categories was only 3.23 percentage points.
- Although the Veggie category recorded the lowest percentage of sales, it still contributed almost one-quarter of total revenue.
- The results indicate that customer demand is fairly diversified across multiple pizza categories rather than being concentrated in a single category.
